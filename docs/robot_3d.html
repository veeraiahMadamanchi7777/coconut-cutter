Why the collar grips

A rubber V-pad sits on one side of the trunk and a steel-cored strap wraps the other. The cantilever arm (350 mm from the trunk axis) tips the collar, so pad and strap bite 100 mm apart. The bite force is the load's moment divided by that gap, so a heavier load grips harder. It holds whenever the lever is at least h / 2μ.

Why there is a tensioner

With the saw swung to the far side of the trunk, its weight cancels most of the tipping moment and the collar would loosen. Before the arm moves out, a self-locking lead screw pulls the upper strap tight (1,000 N rating). It lets go only after the arm is stowed again. Watch the harvest phase: the bunch is on the far side.
